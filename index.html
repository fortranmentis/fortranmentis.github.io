2025.01.14 / TECHNICAL WORKSHOP PRESENTATION

AIS analysis for maritime traffic management.

Presented on January 14, 2025 at the Technical Exchange Workshop for Maritime Traffic Network Operations and Management. The study uses 2022 Busan-area AIS observations to distinguish navigation states and vessel-type activity candidates and display them on a map.

Data and processing

Combine static vessel identifiers/types with time, position, speed over ground, course and heading. The presentation describes Python multiprocessing with ordered imap output.

Navigation-state rules

Combine position, speed, heading and fairway/anchorage context using literature and trajectory-pattern review. Study criteria include speeds below 0.5 knots, arc-like anchoring motion and linear/Z-shaped drifting patterns.

Activity by vessel type

Apply criteria across six broad groups: fishing, towing/tug, yacht/cruise, fast/passenger, cargo and tanker vessels. Indicators use persistent low speed, port/terminal membership and proximity/co-movement.

Presentation settings and follow-up work

The deck describes a one-second display/update interval and a five-minute fairway-exit marker that clears on re-entry. Wind/current integration, richer drifting patterns, per-vessel trajectory displays and accident detection are proposed improvements.

These are research examples from the presentation, not evidence of a currently operating live service or measured classification accuracy. Activity indicators remain rule-based candidates; AIS alone does not confirm an operation or unlawful behavior. Original Esri and other basemap credits are retained in the images.

2021 / COAUTHORED UNDERGRADUATE THESIS

Global mechanisms associated with winter snow depth and snow days in Jeonju

A coauthored 2021 undergraduate thesis from the Climate Modeling Laboratory, Department of Earth and Environmental Sciences, Jeonbuk National University. The study connects local snow observations with global ocean–atmosphere fields and examines statistical relationships and modeled atmospheric responses.

자료 / DATA

1983–2021 Jeonju observations

KMA snow depth and snow-day records, with NOAA SST, wind, 850 hPa temperature, geopotential height and sea ice.

방법 / METHOD

Seasonal means and lagged fields

DJF/three-month means, regression fields at 0–12-month lags and LBM response comparisons. My work used Fortran and GrADS for processing, analysis and visualization.

결과 / FINDINGS

Different signals for depth and frequency

The thesis reports an ENSO association at roughly eight months’ lead for maximum snow depth, with a weaker relation for snow days. LBM experiments compare atmospheric-response patterns.
